# P5: How to Escape the Language Gravity Well

**Published**: April 29, 2026
**Platform**: LinkedIn
**Series**: I Thought, In Reality

---

**Recognizing the Guidance**

You know you're being guided when:
- Your engineering questions get reframed as governance questions
- "How do we build this?" becomes "How should this be regulated?"
- Discussions shift from executable artifacts to abstract frameworks
- You feel the conversation has been "elevated" but nothing's been built

**Stay on the Engineering Path**

Keep asking:
- Decision visibility: How do we make decisions observable?
- Boundary definition: Where does authority start and end?
- Execution traceability: How do we track what actually happened?
- Artifact versioning: How do we know which decision is current?

These aren't governance questions.
They're **engineering questions.**

**Linguistic Compromise Without Intellectual Surrender**

Use the governance vocabulary when necessary.
But don't let it define your scope.

Borrow the words so others can understand you.
But maintain your engineering perspective.

> "Not accepting the governance frame, but discovering: without borrowing these words, the world has no interface to understand what you're doing."

This is linguistic compromise, not intellectual conversion.

**The Real Work**

You're exploring **Decision Infrastructure / Decision Mechanics.**

How decisions are:
- Defined
- Made visible
- Bounded
- Executed
- Recorded

This is **pre-governance work.**

It's about whether decisions even exist in a post-AI world.

Governance is just the shell others wrapped around this problem later.

**Your Action Item**

Next time an AI conversation drifts toward governance:
1. Notice the drift
2. Ask: "What specific engineering structures would make this governable?"
3. Stay focused on the "how" not just the "should"

**Build the structure first.**
**Governance will follow.**
